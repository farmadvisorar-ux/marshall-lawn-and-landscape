Bermuda vs. St. Augustine Grass in East Texas: Which is Best for Your Yard?

In-depth comparison of Bermuda and St. Augustine turfgrass varieties for Marshall, Longview, and Harrison County soil, heat, and shade conditions.

Overview: East Texas Climate & Turf Selection

Selecting the proper turf species is the single most important decision for a healthy lawn in Marshall, Texas (USDA Hardiness Zone 8a). With hot, humid summers reaching 95Â°F+ and mild winters, warm-season grasses reign supreme. The two dominant choices are Hybrid Bermuda (such as Tifway 419 and Celebration) and St. Augustine (such as Palmetto and Raleigh).

Bermuda Grass: The Full-Sun Champion

Bermuda grass is celebrated for its incredible drought tolerance, rapid self-repair via vigorous rhizomes and stolons, and ability to be cut low (1.0 to 1.5 inches) for crisp, athletic turf striping. However, Bermuda strictly requires a minimum of 7 to 8 hours of direct, unfiltered sunlight daily. It will quickly thin and succumb to weeds in shaded areas under mature East Texas pines or oak trees.

St. Augustine Grass: The Shaded Lawn King

St. Augustine features broader, lush blue-green blades and excels in partial shade where Bermuda fails. Cultivars like Palmetto thrive with only 4 to 5 hours of filtered sunlight. St. Augustine should be mowed higher (2.5 to 3.5 inches) to foster deep root establishment and natural weed suppression in Harrison County soils.

Which Variety Should You Install in Marshall, TX?

Choose Bermuda if your property is wide open with full sun exposure and you desire a golf-course style striped lawn with high foot traffic tolerance. Choose St. Augustine if your yard is graced with large canopy trees, shaded side yards, or you prefer a soft, thick, carpet-like lawn.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I mix Bermuda and St. Augustine in the same yard?

A: Yes. Many Marshall properties use Bermuda in full-sun front yards and St. Augustine in shaded, tree-lined backyards.

Q: How often should each grass type be mowed?

A: Both species should be mowed weekly during peak summer growing season to follow the one-third rule and prevent turf stress.
